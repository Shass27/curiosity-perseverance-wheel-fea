\begin{abstract}
\noindent The aluminium skin of the Curiosity rover wheel has cracked in service, with damage concentrated where the thin skin meets the grousers. This report examines a flat segment of that skin, spanning between two grousers and loaded by a rock pressing on a circular patch of radius $a$. A closed-form model, treating the skin as a fixed stepped beam with a Peterson stress concentration at the fillet, is compared with an Ansys 2025 R1 solid model along a path on the skin surface. The beam model reproduces the shape of the FEA stress distribution and the panel-centre peak for sharp contacts ($a = \SI{2}{mm}$, $C = 1.12$). It over-predicts the junction stress by a factor of three to six. For sharp rocks, the centre stress exceeds the \SI{106}{MPa} fatigue limit of the material.
\end{abstract}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}

NASA and JPL mission reports have documented punctures and cracks in the wheels of the Curiosity rover after driving over angular, embedded rocks~\cite{nasa}. The wheel skin is a thin aluminium shell stiffened by chevron-shaped grousers. A rock pressing on the skin between two grousers bends the skin like a plate clamped along its stiffened edges. The bending moment, and hence the surface stress, is largest at two places: directly under the rock, and at the skin--grouser junction, where the section changes abruptly and a small fillet concentrates the stress.

Tier 1 asks a narrow question: \emph{for a given rock sharpness, how large are the junction and centre stresses, and can a hand calculation predict them?} The region studied is marked in Figure~\ref{fig:location}(a). It is idealised as the flat segment shown in Figure~\ref{fig:location}(b), and the patch radius $a$ stands in for rock sharpness: a smaller $a$ is a sharper rock. All CAD, data and scripts are in the project repository~\cite{repo}, and the report links to them rather than reproducing them.

\section{Model and FEA setup}

The segment is a \SI{2}{mm} skin between two grouser blocks, each \SI{5}{mm} wide and \SI{7.5}{mm} deep overall. The skin meets each block through an R\SI{1}{mm} fillet (Table~\ref{tab:inputs}). The two outer end faces are fixed, which represents the continuation of the wheel shell beyond the grousers. The load is a uniform pressure on a circular patch at mid-span, with $a = 2, 4, 6, 8, \SI{10}{mm}$. One \href{\tree/CAD_models/Tier\%201}{CAD file per radius} carries the patch as a split face.

The FEA was solved in Ansys Mechanical 2025 R1 with solid elements at a total force of \SI{300}{N}. Stress was extracted as a \emph{linearized equivalent stress} along a straight path \SI{2}{mm} above the base, which is the top surface of the skin, running across the full \SI{85}{mm} span (Figure~\ref{fig:ansys}). Ansys splits the stress along a path into membrane, bending and peak parts~\cite{ansys}. This report uses the \emph{Total} column, which is the actual von Mises stress at each path point. The analysis load is a design choice of \SI{400}{N}. The model is linear-elastic, so the FEA values are scaled by $400/300$ before any comparison. The \href{\tree/Simulation\%20results/Tier\%201}{exported path data} is in the repository.

\section{Analytical model}

The model follows classical beam and plate theory in the form tabulated by Roark. It is implemented in \href{\blob/scripts\%20and\%20outputs/tier_1/path_stress_analytical.m}{\texttt{path\_stress\_analytical.m}}, and the results are compared in \href{\blob/scripts\%20and\%20outputs/tier_1/fea_vs_analytical.m}{\texttt{fea\_vs\_analytical.m}}.

\subsection{Patch load}
A uniform pressure $P/\pi a^2$ on a circle of radius $a$, integrated across the width, gives a line load along the span $x$:
\begin{equation}
  q(x) = \frac{P}{\pi a^2}\, 2\sqrt{a^2 - (x - x_c)^2}, \qquad |x - x_c| \le a,
\end{equation}
which integrates to $P$. Here $x_c = L/2$ is the patch centre.

\subsection{Fixed stepped beam}
The segment is treated as a beam of width $b$, fixed at $x = 0$ and $x = L$. Its second moment of area $I(x)$ is $bD^3/12$ inside the grouser blocks and $bt^3/12$ over the skin. With $M_q(x)$ the moment of the load to the left of $x$,
\begin{equation}
  M(x) = M_0 + V_0 x - M_q(x),
\end{equation}
and the two unknown end reactions follow from zero slope and zero deflection at the far clamp:
\begin{equation}
  \int_0^L \frac{M}{EI}\,dx = 0, \qquad \int_0^L \frac{M\,(L - x)}{EI}\,dx = 0 .
\end{equation}
This handles the stepped section and any load shape without tables. As $a \to 0$ with rigid grousers it recovers the textbook result $|M| = PL'/8$ at both the ends and the centre of the clear span $L'$. The script checks this limit on every run.

\subsection{Stress on the path}
On the skin surface the bending stress is $\sigma_x = 6M/(bt^2)$, the nominal stress $\sigma_\text{nom}$. At the junction the fillet raises it to
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\text{max} = K_t\, \sigma_\text{nom},
\end{equation}
where $K_t$ is taken from Peterson's fit for a stepped flat bar with a shoulder fillet in bending. With $h/r = 2.75$ and $2h/D = 0.73$, this gives $K_t = 1.63$. The top surface is traction-free ($\sigma_z = 0$), and a wide plate in cylindrical bending is restrained laterally, so $\sigma_y = \nu\sigma_x$. The principal stresses are then $(\sigma_x, \nu\sigma_x, 0)$, and the von Mises stress is
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_\text{vm} = |\sigma_x|\sqrt{1 - \nu + \nu^2}.
\end{equation}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Analytical}
The analytical stress along the path (Figure~\ref{fig:analytical}a) is piecewise linear, as beam theory requires. It peaks at both junctions and under the patch, and passes through zero at the two inflection points near the quarter-spans. Patch size changes only the centre peak, because spreading the load flattens the moment under it, while the junction moment is almost insensitive to $a$ (Table~\ref{tab:summary}). The nominal junction stress of about \SI{120}{MPa} becomes about \SI{195}{MPa} once $K_t$ is applied. Both values exceed the fatigue limit, as do the centre stresses for $a \le \SI{6}{mm}$.

\subsection{FEA}
The FEA stress along the path (Figure~\ref{fig:fea}a) has the same three-peak shape, with minima close to the analytical inflection points. Away from the patch it also falls on the analytical slopes (Figure~\ref{fig:overlays}). The peaks were taken as the largest Total value within \SI{15}{mm} of either end (junction) and within $\pm\SI{10}{mm}$ of mid-span (centre). The centre peak falls from \SI{129}{MPa} at $a = \SI{2}{mm}$ to \SI{42}{MPa} at $a = \SI{10}{mm}$. The junction peaks stay between 30 and \SI{65}{MPa} (Figure~\ref{fig:fea}b).

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Panel centre.}
For the sharpest contact the beam model is close: $C_c = 1.12$ at $a = \SI{2}{mm}$, a 12\% under-prediction. That is expected, because the beam spreads the load over the full \SI{40}{mm} width while the real plate carries it more locally. As $a$ grows, the FEA centre stress drops much faster than the beam predicts. This is the plate effect again: a wide patch is shared in two directions, which a one-dimensional strip cannot capture. The $a = \SI{4}{mm}$ case ($C_c = 0.33$) breaks the otherwise monotonic trend and should be re-checked, most likely for the path position relative to the patch or the mesh under it.

\paragraph{Junction.}
The analytical junction stress is three to six times the FEA value ($C_j = 0.15$--$0.33$). Several simplifications act in the same direction:
(i) the grouser is assumed to clamp the skin rigidly, whereas the \SI{5}{mm} block itself rotates and relieves the junction moment;
(ii) Peterson's chart is for a symmetric shoulder, which is more severe than the one-sided T-junction here;
(iii) the moment is averaged over the full width, which over-states the moment carried at the junction line far from the patch;
(iv) the FEA path samples every \SI{1.77}{mm}, so it can step over the fillet, which is only \SI{1}{mm} long, and miss the true local peak.
The overlays support (i) and (iv): the FEA rises towards the junction on the analytical slope but stops short of it, and peaks a few millimetres inside the span.

\paragraph{Implications.}
Even with these corrections, both models agree that a sharp rock ($a \le \SI{2}{mm}$) at \SI{400}{N} drives the skin centre above the \SI{106}{MPa} fatigue limit. Under repeated loading this location will initiate cracks before the junction.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusions}

A fixed stepped-beam model with a Peterson stress concentration reproduces the shape of the FEA stress distribution along the skin and, for sharp contacts, the centre peak to within about 10\%. It is conservative at the junction by a factor of three to six, mainly because it treats the grouser as a rigid clamp and uses a symmetric-shoulder $K_t$. Sharp rocks ($a \approx \SI{2}{mm}$) push the centre stress above the fatigue limit. Tier 2 will refine the path sampling at the fillet, re-check the $a = \SI{4}{mm}$ case, and use the calibration factors to map safe designs against rock sharpness, fillet radius and skin thickness.
